# Doom at 640x480 - what was measured

Question: can the Doom port render at 640x480 and stay fast?

Short answer: the renderer scales to 640x480 and the machine can display it, but **no**, not at
60 frames per second, and the port is not ready to ship that mode. Two separate problems:

1. The 3D renderer's cost is proportional to pixels, and 640x480 is four times the pixels.
2. Everything that is not the 3D view (status bar, HUD, menus, intermission, wipes, the loading
   screen) is artwork and layout hard-wired to 320x200. It does not scale, lands in the wrong
   place, and some of it aborts on a 480-line screen.

## Measured at 320x240 (the shipping mode)

Profiled with `NEMUL_PROF`, window 8000..12000 ms of the attract demo, 136 displayed frames:

```
instructions 310,037,962   modelled cycles 582,326,449
2,279,691 instructions per frame   4,281,812 cycles per frame

R_DrawColumn      46.1%   R_DrawSpan  18.8%   I_UpdateSound  5.7%
R_RenderSegLoop    3.8%   memcpy       3.3%   P_GroupLines   2.4%
```

So about 2.28M instructions per frame, of which 65% is the two rasterisers. On a 300MHz
ARMv5TE that is roughly 7.6ms of issue time per frame against a 16.7ms budget, before SDRAM
stalls; the port runs at the console's 60Hz rate in emulated time.

Per pixel: (0.65 * 2.28M) / 76,800 pixels ~= 19 instructions per pixel.

## What was tried at 640x480

The port now has compile-time switches in `src/examples/ports/freedoom1`:

- `-DPVMK_640X480` - `SCREENWIDTH/HEIGHT` 640x480, the VGA framebuffer, and `screenblocks`
  default 11 (with 10, Doom draws a 320-wide window centred in the 640-wide buffer, which is
  why a naive resolution change looks like a small view in the middle).
- `-DPVMK_AUTODEMO` - start the first attract demo immediately instead of the title screen.
- `-DPVMK_NOHUD` - skip the 320x200 HUD/status bar/menus/wipes, for renderer benchmarking.

Verified working:

- The game enters `GS_LEVEL` with `demoplayback=1`, `viewwidth=640`, `viewheight=480`, and
  flips 640x480 buffers (nemul logs `MODE 1`).
- `R_DrawColumn` is reached with `dc_x=352, dc_yl=81, dc_yh=295` and `ylookup` pointing inside
  the 640x480 buffer, i.e. the renderer is set up for the larger screen.

Not working:

- The status bar and HUD are laid out for 320x200. `STlib_drawNum` aborts with
  `drawNum: n->y - ST_Y < 0`; `V_DrawPatch` rejects patches centred for the wider screen
  (`Patch at 368,-3 exceeds LFB`) and some of those paths print once per frame, which is slow
  enough to dominate a profiled run.
- The loading screen copies 320x240 rows into a 640-stride buffer, so it appears in the left
  half; it is a startup graphic, not part of the frame loop.
- A full, isolated 640x480 renderer number was not obtained: the HUD-free build reached the
  demo but the profiled window was dominated by the sound pump and the world renderer's cost
  could not be separated from the game's state in the time available. Treat the projection
  below as an estimate, not a measurement.

## The projection

Rasteriser cost at 640x480 is 4x the pixel count: 0.65 * 2.28M * 4 ~= 5.9M instructions per
frame, plus ~0.8M for the rest of the game ~= 6.7M instructions per frame. That is ~22ms of
issue time at 300 MIPS, before SDRAM traffic - and the scanout itself reads 614KB per frame
instead of 154KB. A realistic estimate is 20-25 frames per second, i.e. the game would have to
be played at half speed or the renderer would have to get about 3x cheaper per pixel.

## What a real 640x480 mode would need

1. A 2x HUD/status-bar/menu scaler (or redraw the HUD at native resolution), and 2x wipe and
   finale paths. Until that exists, 640x480 is a 3D viewport change, not a game mode.
2. Rasteriser work: Doom's column and span drawers are already the 65% hot spot. The wins
   available there are the same as at 320x240 - wider stores, fewer per-pixel palette lookups,
   less work per column - and they buy a few percent, not 3x.
3. Fewer pixels, not more: keep the internal render at 320x240 and scale the view up with a
   cheap 2x2 blit if a 640x480 picture is wanted. That costs 76,800 extra framebuffer reads and
   307,200 writes per frame (about 0.3M instructions if done a word at a time) instead of 4x
   the whole rasteriser, and it keeps the HUD and all artwork at their native size.

The last option is the one worth building if a 640x480 Doom is wanted: a 2x2 pixel-doubling
scanout of the existing 320x240 buffer. It is four times cheaper than rendering at 640x480 and
it needs no artwork changes.
